# Wear OS face builders leave a documented gap

**Builder apps for Wear OS faces exist, but no source I could find shows anyone offering the full set you propose: free colour values, user-movable complications, user-chosen ring complications (full, halves, thirds, quarters) and photo backgrounds, in one guided phone editor.** Pujie, WatchMaker, TIMEFLIK, Facer Creator and the new Recreative "AllFaces" all market some form of "design your own face", and the Watch Face Push pipeline you plan (phone generates a face with Google's Pack, a validator token, push to the watch) is proven by Google's own Androidify app. Photo backgrounds are already table stakes. The gap claim is weaker than it sounds, though. It rests on absent documentation, not on hands-on audits, and no commercial editor was tested. The platform has no policy barrier I could find, but the market is small, flat and Samsung-dependent, and nobody has published earnings figures for indie face developers. **Verdict: build it only as a small, time-boxed bet, and run a two-week validation (a hands-on audit of competitors and a device spike) before committing to the full scope.** It is a credible differentiated tool, but the evidence does not support expecting a large commercial return.

## Builders exist, but the claimed feature set is undocumented everywhere

Five commercial products pitch end-user face creation. Facer (10M+ installs, 4.3 stars, updated 7 Oct 2026) has a web-based Creator and a phone app that syncs faces to the watch ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jeremysteckling.facerrel)). WatchMaker (5M+) advertises an in-app editor for "colors, layouts, hands, complications, fonts, backgrounds" ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=slide.watchFrenzy)). Pujie (100K+, 4.5 stars) sells a "full custom watch face editor" ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pujie.watchfaces)). TIMEFLIK (10M+) lets users build faces from photos and templates in its phone app ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.apposter.watchmaker)). Recreative's AllFaces (50K+) added a from-scratch builder in 2026 ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.recreative.recreativefaces)). Google named Facer, TIMEFLIK, WatchMaker, Pujie and Recreative as design partners for Watch Face Push ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/05/whats-new-in-watch-faces.html)).

The notes' researchers could not confirm free hex input, numeric sliders, repositionable complications or arc authoring in any of them, because the editor details are not on the listings. Treat that as a gap, not proof of absence. The platform's own editor is clearly limited. Watch Face Format (WFF) offers only boolean, list and fixed colour-theme configurations, and the colour theme is a designer-defined list of 1 to 100 options ([Define user configurations](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/wff/personalization/user-configurations), [ColorConfiguration reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/wear-os/wff/user-configuration/color-configuration)). Samsung states that edge complication slots cannot be moved, resized or rotated ([Samsung codelab](https://developer.samsung.com/codelab/watch-face-studio/edge-complication-digital-clock.html)). Ring partitioning is fixed by the face author everywhere I looked: Samsung's Duo (halves) and Quadrant (quarters) edge slots, and Pixel's Track "Double" face that splits the ring in half ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/09/13/pixel-watch-3-new-faces/)). I found no example of thirds and no editor where the end user chooses the split. That is a real vocabulary gap, but the catalogues of Facer and WatchMaker (100K+ faces each) were not searched for such faces.

## The technical route is proven, and the closest open-source rival is embryonic

Google's Androidify app builds a WFF face on the phone with Pack, validates it, streams it over the Data Layer and calls `addWatchFace` or `updateWatchFace` on the watch ([Android Developers Blog, Dec 2025](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/12/bringing-androidify-to-wear-os-with.html)). The `androidx.wear.watchfacepush` library hit 1.0.0 stable on 2026-04-08, and its release notes describe the use case "a user creates or edits a watch face on their phone" ([release notes](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/wear-watchfacepush)). That is an endorsement of your architecture, not just tolerance for it.

The open-source field is thin. The nearest project is `miopea/bfg-watchfaces` (Apache-2.0, created 2026-08-27, 0 stars), which uses Pack and Push to generate parametric dials with five fixed complication slots and an importable image. By its own commit log it is on the Play internal track, with a readiness guard that wrongly blocked a Pixel Watch 5 and watch-reply paths that "have never executed on hardware" ([commit](https://github.com/miopea/bfg-watchfaces/commit/5c4b62daf9ccdfd9ac69a749625578deeeb32099)). Its SPEC is the most useful public document for pitfalls: colours must be `#AARRGGBB` or they are silently wrong, a schema-invalid face installs and then never appears, ambient mode is a per-element variant, and AGP-built APKs include files Push rejects ([SPEC](https://github.com/miopea/bfg-watchfaces/blob/main/docs/SPEC.md)). A browser tool, `wfpush-studio`, pushes hand-written XML over Wi-Fi and is not a consumer editor ([repo](https://github.com/jamesbiederbeck/wfpush-studio)). No open-source visual WFF editor exists, so a good editor would be greenfield. Open-source editors for Xiaomi and Amazfit are UX references only ([EasyFace](https://github.com/m0tral/EasyFace)).

The enabling libraries are young. `google/pack` has 235 stars and is "not an officially supported Google product". Its recent commits are dependency bumps, and it supports only single-language strings and plain `drawable` folders ([README](https://github.com/google/pack/blob/main/README.md)). The Push validator is still `1.0.0-alpha10` ([release notes](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/wear-watchfacepush)). The hard rules shape the product, and all come from Google's guides ([Watch Face Push guide](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/watch-face-push), [Wear OS app guide](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/watch-face-push/wear-os-app)):

- Wear OS 6 allows **one slot per app**.
- `setWatchFaceAsActive` works **only once**, and a denied permission cannot be re-requested.
- The package name must follow `<app>.watchfacepush.<name>`.
- The APK may contain only manifest, resources and `res/**`.
- Faces must be signed with a key different from the app's key.
- Every face needs a validator token.
- A watch-side app is mandatory, because the API can only be called there.

Two inferences follow. The face library has to live on the phone and be re-pushed on selection. Any "free value" is baked in at generation time, so each edit means regenerate, validate and push over Bluetooth, and Google advises compressing assets for that reason. Your bundled default face maps to the documented `assets/default_watchface.apk` mechanism, which is not auto-updated when the app updates. I could not verify the phone-side minimum Android version for Pack's native libraries, so "Android 9+" is untested.

## Policy shows no blocker, but the evidence is negative, not confirmed

No Watch Face Push page states an application, allowlist or approval gate, and nothing prohibits phone-side generation. The page served at `/watch-face-push-policies` is the same technical guide with no policy text ([Google](https://developer.android.com/watch-face-push-policies)). The standard Play rules apply ([Transparency Center](https://transparency.google/intl/en_us/our-policies/product-terms/wear-os/)). The one policy where a reviewer might ask questions is the ban on downloading executable code ([Device and Network Abuse](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888379)). Generated faces are code-free (`hasCode="false"`), but no Google statement confirms that reading (**inference**). User-generated-content rules would apply only if you add sharing, and Play does not scan pushed APKs, so quality is your responsibility ([Watch Face Push guide](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/watch-face-push)). New personal developer accounts need a 12-tester, 14-day closed test before production ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)).

Two gaps matter here. The official `WatchFacePushManager` reference returned 404, so I could not confirm the protection level of the `PUSH_WATCH_FACES` permission. Facer and Androidify do use the API, and that is the best available evidence that it is open. The Play Console review treatment of Push apps was also not found. A closed-test upload is the cheapest way to settle both. Separately, one more risk applies to every third-party face: a Wear OS 6 / 6.1 always-on-display "ghosting" bug on Pixel and Galaxy watches, tracked by Google since October 2025. Its current status is unknown ([PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/display-bug-breaking-third-party-watch-faces-on-pixel-and-galaxy-watches_id176990)). Ambient behaviour needs explicit testing.

## Users complain about discontinuity and one-slot friction, not about missing colour pickers

The best-evidenced complaints concern what the Wear OS 5/6 move to WFF broke. Facer forum users report purchased faces showing "Not Available", lost shortcuts and phone-battery data, no preview before purchase, and complications that must be reconfigured every time a face changes ([Facer community](https://community.facer.io/t/wearos-6-need-to-reconfig-complications-on-face-change/101502), [thread](https://community.facer.io/t/upgrading-to-wearos6-options/101989)). Facer's Trustpilot score is **1.9/5 from 19 self-selected reviews**, dominated by billing and lost-purchase complaints ([Trustpilot](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/facer.io)). Android Central's hands-on found a split Premium-versus-VIP price on WatchMaker that felt like paying twice, and ads before every send on TIMEFLIK ([Android Central via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/wearables/articles/tested-best-watch-face-apps-150400676.html)). Pujie draws discoverability complaints: a May 2026 reviewer could not find how to start a new face ([Play reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pujie.watchfaces)). Its designer is described as complex ([Android Central via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/wearables/articles/tested-best-watch-face-apps-150400676.html)).

Two implications are **inferences**. First, "design persists and survives switching" and a guided wizard are day-one requirements, since the single-slot rule makes you inherit Facer's reset complaint unless you store choices on the phone. Second, a one-off or low-price model avoids the loudest billing complaints. I found **no direct user requests** for free colour pickers, ring complications or movable elements. Reddit and Play review text were unreachable, so demand for the exact feature set is inferred, not documented. A feature that matters in the opposite direction is photos: Google added user photos in WFF v4 and Samsung added a Photo slot in 2026 ([WFF release notes](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/wff/release-notes)), so photos are expected, not a selling point.

## The market is small and flat, and income evidence is thin

Google publishes no absolute Wear OS install base. Counterpoint reported Wear OS shipments falling year on year through 2025 because of Samsung, with a recovery expected from Pixel Watch 4 ([Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/reports/global-smartwatch-shipment-forecast-by-operating-system-october-2025-update), summary text only). Samsung held 5% of global smartwatch shipments in Q1 2026, down from 7% ([Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/?p=1290915)). Watch Face Push needs Wear OS 6 (Android 16), and Wear OS 7 was announced in May 2026 with Pixel rollout under way ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/19/google-announces-wear-os-7/)), so Wear OS 6 is a floor, not a ceiling. One discrepancy: BFG's SPEC says Pixel Watch 1 to 3 cannot run Push, while 9to5Google's guide says Pixel Watch 2 and 3 received Wear OS 6 in October 2025 ([guide](https://9to5google.com/guides/wear-os-6/)). The original Pixel Watch never gets it, so your device matrix needs checking on real hardware.

Maker-first apps are two orders of magnitude smaller than catalogues: Pujie's 100K+ against Facer's and TIMEFLIK's 10M+ (**inference**: most users want a catalogue). A study of 386 fitness smartwatch users found most either customize frequently or not at all ([Gouveia and Epstein](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/this-watchface-fits-with-my-tattoos-investigating-customisation-n/), abstract via search), which suggests a small, intense audience. Pricing anchors are $39.99 a year for Facer and WatchMaker, about $5 a month, and about $5 one-off for Pujie per Android Central in September 2025. Facer's only quantified designer income is "over $1 million" cumulative to 75+ designers by December 2021 ([Facer](https://news.facer.io/celebrating-1-million-in-designer-payouts-and-launching-our-first-facer-creator-partner-program/)). Indie developer anecdotes from Samsung's forum report very low paid conversion, one sale against 600+ free downloads, but those are single stories dated 2019 to 2022 ([Samsung forum](https://forum.developer.samsung.com/t/sales-are-very-low/8457)). Play fees are 20% plus 5% on one-off sales and 10% plus 5% on subscriptions in the US, UK and EEA from 2026-06-30, though the source table was ambiguous ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)).

Platform competition is real but narrow. Pixel Watch 5's "Create" feature (Aug 2026) generates static faces from a prompt in three fixed layouts, could not make an analog face, and is not confirmed on Galaxy ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=726965), [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2241496/google-pixel-watch-5-review), via search summary). The direction, Gemini plus WFF, is the longer-term threat to casual customization. A deterministic analog-face builder is the less exposed end.

## Verdict, risks and what to validate first

Build it, scoped as an experiment, not as a business plan. The technical feasibility is established, the policy path looks open, and a differentiated feature set is plausible. Against that, the audience is flat, income evidence is absent, incumbents have catalogue scale, and your key claims are unverified. The strongest case is an analog-focused tool that does what the stock editors cannot: ring partition choice (including thirds, which has no precedent in the notes), free colours, positioning and a wizard, with the phone as the home of the saved design.

The main risks, in order, are these:

- **Differentiation risk.** One incumbent may already offer part of this, and Pujie, WatchMaker and AllFaces were never tested hands-on.
- **Market-size risk.** The Wear OS 6+ user base is small and Samsung-dependent.
- **Platform-churn risk.** Wear OS 7 and One UI 9 Watch arrive this year, the Push validator is alpha, and Pack is lightly maintained.
- **Rendering and ambient risk.** The AOD ghosting bug and WFF's declarative limits cap what you can promise.
- **Unconfirmed access risk.** Permission protection levels and Play review of Push apps are unconfirmed.
- **Monetisation risk.** There is no evidence that indie face tools earn meaningful money.

| Validate first | How | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| Competitor hands-on audit | Install Pujie, WatchMaker, TIMEFLIK and AllFaces free tiers on a Wear OS 6 watch or emulator, and check hex input, sliders, drag-to-move and arcs | Whether the gap exists at all |
| End-to-end device spike | Pack on a phone, validator-push-android, push to a Galaxy Watch 8 and a Pixel Watch, with a free-position complication and a three-segment ring | Technical feasibility, Android 9 support, ambient rendering, push latency |
| Access and policy check | Upload to a closed test early, confirm the permission works on a retail device and Play review accepts it | The unconfirmed policy items |
| Demand test | Ask in watch-face communities for the specific features and measure willingness to pay at a one-off price | The demand and pricing inference |

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "has anyone built this?" to "does anyone offer this combination, and does it matter to enough people?" The first half has a tentative answer: partial builders exist, the specific combination of user-chosen ring splits, free values and movable complications is undocumented anywhere, and the architecture is Google-endorsed. The second half has no evidence for or against, which is the real risk. Users complain about continuity and billing, so persistence of the design and a plain pricing model may matter more than any single editor control.

The most actionable finding is how cheap the uncertainty is to retire. A competitor audit and a device spike answer the differentiation and feasibility questions in days, before the editor, onboarding and the ring and photo work are committed. Whether the product earns money remains unanswered by any available data, and the plan should assume it will not until a demand test says otherwise.
